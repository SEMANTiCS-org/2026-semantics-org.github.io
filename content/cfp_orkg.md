The [Open Research Knowledge Graph](https://orkg.org/) (ORKG) and the organizing committee of SEMANTiCS 2026 are collaborating to promote best practices in research data management. The ORKG is a collaborative research knowledge graph that enables researchers to describe research artifacts in a structured manner. To encourage best practices in artifact publishing, authors of SEMANTiCS contributions have the opportunity to submit ORKG content related to their research presented at SEMANTiCS. For each submission, ORKG badges are added to the SEMANTiCS website, linking the publication to the corresponding ORKG content and highlighting the authors’ commitment to best practices in research data publishing. Furthermore, the highest-quality ORKG submissions will be awarded prize money, as outlined in the table above.

## Types of Submission

The ORKG supports various content types, in this call we specifically invite submissions for: (1) ORKG Comparisons with visualizations, (2) ORKG Reviews and ORKG Lists, and (3) ORKG Datasets, Software and Papers. Each of these content types can be described in a structured manner within the ORKG and published once ready.

### ORKG Comparisons with visualizations

ORKG Comparisons organize research contributions in a tabular format, offering a clear overview of a research field, and can be enhanced with visualizations such as bar charts or scatter plots for a more intuitive understanding of the data. Specifically, Visualizations help with numeric data and can improve the understanding of a Comparison. However, Visualizations are not suitable for each Comparison and are therefore optional when creating Comparisons.

### ORKG Reviews and ORKG Lists

ORKG Reviews use other artifacts from the ORKG to create a living review document. This includes Comparisons, Visualizations, etc., and can be extended with natural text, to provide context for the review, helping human readers. ORKG Lists simply present curated lists of related work, grouped by category. While not containing much structured data per se, they can be helpful for readers to quickly find state-of-the-art work or serve as a starting point for Comparisons.

### ORKG Datasets, Software and Papers

ORKG Datasets and Software provide structured metadata descriptions along with links to the original artifacts. Finally, ORKG Papers describe the findings of individual publications in a structured form. All of the aforementioned artifacts can be published with a DOI, ensuring that they can be properly cited in research articles.

## Submission guidelines

- To be eligible for prize money, ORKG content must be linked to accepted SEMANTiCS submissions in the following categories: (1) Research and Innovation Track, (2) Posters and Demos Track, or (3) accepted workshops. During the submission, you are required to specify the track (or workshop name) and the submission ID.
- The submitted ORKG content must be directly related to the work presented at SEMANTiCS. For example, an ORKG Comparison may be based on the related work discussed in the publication; an ORKG Paper may describe the authors’ submission in a structured manner; or an ORKG Dataset or Software resource may document data or software developed as part of the published research.
- All submitted ORKG content must have a DOI assigned. DOIs can be assigned when publishing ORKG content types. An exception is made for ORKG Lists, they cannot be published with a DOI, provide the ORKG URL instead during submission.
- The deadline for the ORKG call for content is **August 31, 2026, at 23:59 AoE**. This deadline is after the camera-ready deadlines of all SEMANTiCS tracks, allowing authors to submit ORKG resources after they have been notified of their paper’s acceptance.

## All ORKG Content Types

Beyond the types of submission proposed within this call, here we list all the allowed ORKG content types:

- ORKG Comparisons: tabular overview of how related literature compares to each other. Generally, the comparison table either compares the authors' own research with state-of-the-art work, or structures articles from the related work section. Examples of ORKG Comparisons: [Example comparison](https://orkg.org/comparisons/R642234), [Example comparison 2](https://orkg.org/comparisons/R598411), [Example comparison 3](https://orkg.org/comparisons/R44930).
- ORKG Reviews: ORKG reviews are dynamic, community maintained scholarly articles and are especially suitable for survey papers: [Example review](https://orkg.org/reviews/R135360), [Example review 2](https://orkg.org/reviews/R606854).
- ORKG Lists: Literature lists for a specific research domain: [Example list](https://orkg.org/lists/R1385252).
- ORKG Papers: in-depth structured descriptions of individual research articles, specifically the article published at SEMANTiCS. [Example paper](https://orkg.org/papers/R8186).
- ORKG Software: link to source code, creators, license, publication date, programming language, platform, etc. [Example software](https://orkg.org/content-type/Software/R1568469).
- ORKG Dataset: link to data repository, name, description, license, size, encoding, etc. [Example dataset](https://orkg.org/content-type/Dataset/R1568117).

To start adding your research data to ORKG, visit [this page](orkg.org/add). For more information about creating ORKG content types, visit the [ORKG academy](https://academy.orkg.org/orkg-academy/main/tutorials.html).

## FAQ

**How does the awarding of the prize money work?**

- The prize money can be transferred to any bank account with an IBAN number. The details will be clarified once the prize is awarded.

**Can I link ORKG content types in my publications?**

- It is definitely a good idea, and even best practice, to link to the created ORKG artifacts within your publication. However, specifically for tracks with a double-blind submission process, please be aware you cannot disclose your identity while doing so. We recommend you to either add such links to your articles when preparing the camera-ready version of your paper or use the anonymization feature when publishing the Comparison during submission.
- ORKG Comparisons can be published anonymously when selecting the conference 'SEMANTiCS 2026' when clicking the 'Edit metadata' button while being in edit mode for a specific comparison. After the notification deadline, the creators will be made visible automatically. Adding ORKG Comparisons during the submission is optional and does not change the submission process for this special track for ORKG resources, as described above. Specifically, ORKG Comparisons are also valuable during the review process, as it helps reviewers to compare your approach to the state-of-the-art. Therefore, it can be a good idea to already link an ORKG comparison during the submission.

**How are submissions reviewed?**

- Submissions are reviewed by an ORKG jury that assesses submissions based on several criteria, including: quality, relevancy and novelty. Reviews will not be shared with the authors and are only used to determine which submissions should be awarded the prize money. While ORKG submissions can go through multiple iterations based on feedback from the ORKG team, the jury will only assess the first submitted version for the “best content type” prize to ensure fairness in the competition.

**Can I submit multiple ORKG content types?**

- Yes, it is possible to, for example, submit an ORKG Comparison, ORKG Paper and ORKG Software. However, authors can only receive a single prize. For example, it is not possible to get both the prize for the best ORKG Paper and the best ORKG Software description.
