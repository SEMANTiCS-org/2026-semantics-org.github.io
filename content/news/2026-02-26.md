# Ghent!

##### February 26, 2026 by SEMANTiCS

<img src="../img/news/2026_02_26.png" style="" width="100%" height="auto" alt="">

You might wonder why this city has not been the scene for the SEMANTiCS conference before. It is a city full of history on consensus making and innovation that we hold so dearly that couldn’t be more relevant.

The [castle of the counts](https://historischehuizen.stad.gent/en/castle-counts) for example is a symbol from medieval times for early consensus making mechanisms that would be called unconventional today: repression. Ghentians, known for being stubborn, were to make sure they walked the line, or they would feel the consequences. For some, a stark reminder to some web standardization meetings they’ve been in. Later in history the castle got a reputation boost, as it got occupied in 1949 by students demanding lower beer prices.

In St Bavo’s Cathedral, you can visit [Jan van Eyck’s altarpiece ‘The Adoration of the Mystic Lamb’ (1432)](https://www.sintbaafskathedraal.be/nl/geschiedenis/lam-gods/). With innovative techniques at the time, [Jan van Eyck](https://visit.gent.be/en/jan-van-eyck-master-and-genius) brought about an optical revolution. With his unequalled technique and observational skills, he elevated oil painting to new heights and shaped the course of fine arts. Since 1934 one of the panels has been missing as it was stolen. There could hardly be a better illustration of the open world assumption: keep your eyes peeled when walking through Ghent, you might always encounter another panel of the altarpiece.

In 1810, Ghent became a key starting point of the mechanised cotton industry on the European continent. Not with innovation of our own, but thanks to Lieven Bauwens, who smuggled the Mule Jenny from the UK to Ghent. You’ll be able to check out the Mule Jenny with your own eyes as it’s on display in the [Museum of Industry](https://www.industriemuseum.be/en).

In 2014, the Knowledge on Web-Scale team at Ghent University positioned the idea of [Linked Data Fragments](https://linkeddatafragments.org/). Similar to the Mule Jenny, some would dare to call this not an innovation of our own, but just a translation of the divide-and-conquer strategy commonly applied in data technologies. Others may compare it to the Jan van Eyck altarpiece, as the longer you wait, the more cunning details you’ll discover.

Nowadays, the city of Ghent is called a hidden gem for tourists, but also the [technology capital of Belgium](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/05/21/techsector-technologie-gent-wintercircus-start-up-unicorns-dealr/) with a vibrant start-up ecosystem. It also hosts our administration [Digital Flanders](https://www.vlaanderen.be/digitaal-vlaanderen), which is a true front-runner in interoperable govtech. For example, they maintain [data.vlaanderen.be](http://data.vlaanderen.be): the standard vocabularies and application profiles to be used when you interface with the government. Also Flanders’ data utility company Athumi is active in Ghent, and together we are building the [Trustflows.eu](http://Trustflows.eu) community. In the south of Ghent, we’re now building a [new AI campus hub](https://idlab.ugent.be/resources/ai-campus-hub), to also bring together all AI researchers in a new building called “The Brain” starting 2027. In case you’re looking for an office in Ghent, we’re still looking for companies to join us there!

The city of Ghent itself is well-versed in Linked Data. You can visit the SPARQL endpoint at [https://stad.gent/sparql](https://stad.gent/sparql).
